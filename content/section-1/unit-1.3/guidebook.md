# Unit 1.3 — Check, mate and draws

## The habit

**In check? Move, block, or take. Before the last square goes, make sure it is check.**

## Check

Your king is in **check** when an enemy piece attacks the square he is standing
on. You are never allowed to leave him there, and you are never allowed to move
him onto a square that is attacked. A check is not a polite suggestion: you must
answer it with your very next move.

## The three ways out

1. **Move** the king to a square nothing attacks.
2. **Block** the line, by putting one of your own pieces between the king and
   the attacker. This only works against a queen, a rook or a bishop, because
   only they attack along a line. A knight's check cannot be blocked.
3. **Take** the piece that is giving check.

If two pieces are checking at once, blocking or taking one of them still leaves
the other. In that case the king has to move.

## Checkmate

**Checkmate** is check where none of the three ways out is left. The king is
attacked, every square he could go to is covered, nothing can get in the way,
and nothing can capture the attacker. The game stops immediately. Nobody takes
the king off the board.

To hunt for mate, list the moves that give check. For each one, ask the three
questions in order — can he move, can anything block, can anything take? The
check where all three answers are no is mate.

## Stalemate, and why it is not mate

**Stalemate** is the one to watch. The player to move is **not in check**, and
has **no legal move at all**. That is a draw. Half a point each, whatever the
material on the board.

The difference is one question, and it is always the same question:

| | Is the king attacked? | Has he a legal move? | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Checkmate | yes | no | the attacker wins |
| Stalemate | no | no | draw |
| Check | yes | yes | play on |

So when you are winning and the enemy king is down to his last square, do not
take that square away with a quiet move. Take it away **with check**. One square
is the whole difference between the full point and half of it.

## The three draws

- **Stalemate** — no check, no legal move.
- **Not enough material** — king against king, king and one bishop against king,
  king and one knight against king. Nobody can force mate, so the game is drawn.
- **The same position three times** — when the identical position, with the same
  player to move, happens three times, either player may claim a draw.

Players may also agree a draw between themselves, and there is a fifty-move rule
that unit 1.4 covers along with the rest of the written rules.
