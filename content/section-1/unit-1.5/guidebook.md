# Unit 1.5 — Your first mates

## The habit

**Drive the king to an edge, then deliver.**

Every mate in this unit is the same two-part idea. A king in the middle of the
board has eight squares around him and you will never cover them all. A king on
the edge has five, and a king in a corner has three. So you do not hunt for a
mate — you take space away until there is almost nothing left, and only then do
you give the check.

## Taking the space away

A rook or a queen owns a whole rank or a whole file at once. Put one across the
board behind the enemy king and he can never come back through it. That is the
only tool you need:

- **Two rooks.** One holds a rank, the other checks on the next rank along.
  Then they swap jobs. The king walks backwards one rank at a time until the
  eighth rank runs out. This is the ladder.
- **Queen and rook.** The same ladder, only faster, because the queen counts as
  a rook for this job.
- **Queen alone.** She takes squares away by herself, but she cannot give the
  final check without help, so your king has to walk up and guard the mating
  square.
- **Rook alone.** The rook draws the line, your king walks up until the two
  kings face each other with one square between them, and then the rook checks
  along the edge.

## The two ways it goes wrong

**Stalemate.** If you take the enemy king's last square and you are *not*
giving check, the game is a draw and a whole winning position is gone. This is
the one real danger in king and queen against king, because the queen covers so
much. Before every move with her, ask: will he still have a square? If not,
give check instead, or bring your own king up first.

**Forgetting your own back rank.** A king behind three untouched pawns is safe
from nearly everything and completely trapped by a rook or queen arriving on his
own first rank. That cuts both ways. When you see it in their position, take it.
When it is in yours, spend one quiet pawn move and give your king a square.

## Two mates that arrive early

Some opponents skip all of this and go straight for **f7** — the one square on
your side that only your king defends. A queen and a bishop both aiming at it is
Scholar's mate coming. Count the attackers and the defenders the way you did in
unit 1.2: two against one means you must act now. Cover f7, or chase the queen
off, or take her if she has come out where you can reach her.

**Fool's mate** is the opposite: nobody does it to you, you do it to yourself by
moving both pawns in front of your own king in the first two moves and opening
the road to h4. Leave those pawns alone.

## Before you start hunting

1. Which edge is the king nearest?
2. What line can I draw behind him?
3. Does my king need to come and help?
4. After my move, does he still have a square?
