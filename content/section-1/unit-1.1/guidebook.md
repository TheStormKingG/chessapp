# 1.1 The board and the pieces

Chess is played on 64 squares, and every one of them has a name: the file letter
first, a to h from White's left, then the rank number, 1 to 8 counting away from
White. Six kinds of piece move across those squares, and each one moves in
exactly one way that never changes for the whole game. Most of them slide until
something is in the way; the knight jumps and ignores whatever it jumps over;
the pawn is the odd one, walking one way and taking another. A piece takes by
landing on an enemy piece's square, which means knowing how a piece moves is the
same as knowing what it can take. Learn these six patterns and nothing else on
the board is a surprise.

## The six movements

- **Rook** — straight lines: along its rank and up and down its file, any
  distance, until something blocks it.
- **Bishop** — diagonals, any distance, and it stays on its starting colour for
  the whole game.
- **Queen** — a rook and a bishop in one: straight lines and diagonals, any
  distance.
- **King** — one square in any of the eight directions, and never onto a square
  an enemy piece attacks.
- **Knight** — two squares one way then one square across, landing in an L, and
  nothing in between can block it.
- **Pawn** — forward one square, or two from its starting row; it takes one
  square diagonally forward, and it turns into a piece of your choice on reaching
  the far end.

## Setting up

Turn the board so the square in your right-hand corner is light. Rooks in the
corners, knights beside them, bishops next, then the queen on the square of her
own colour and the king on the one left over. Pawns fill the row in front.
