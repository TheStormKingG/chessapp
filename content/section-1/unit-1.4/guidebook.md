# Unit 1.4 — Castling and the rules of play

## The habit

**Castle as soon as possible.**

The king is safest tucked behind his own pawns, and the rook beside him is
worth far more than the rook asleep in the corner. Castling does both jobs in
one move, so do it early rather than one move too late.

## Castling, both sides

| Side  | Written | King    | Rook    | Must be empty  |
| ----- | ------- | ------- | ------- | -------------- |
| Short | O-O     | e1 → g1 | h1 → f1 | f1, g1         |
| Long  | O-O-O   | e1 → c1 | a1 → d1 | b1, c1, d1     |

Black does the same on the eighth row: e8 → g8 or e8 → c8.

## The four conditions

All four must be true, every time:

1. **The king has never moved.** Once he moves, castling is gone for good,
   even if he walks straight back to e1.
2. **That rook has never moved.** Each rook carries its own permission. Move
   the h1 rook and you can still castle long.
3. **The squares between them are empty.** On the long side that is three
   squares, not two.
4. **The king is not in check, does not cross an attacked square, and does not
   land on one.** The rook may pass an attacked square; the king may not.

## En passant

A pawn that jumps two squares and lands beside an enemy pawn may be taken as
if it had gone one square only. The capturing pawn lands on the square that
was skipped and the jumper comes off.

Two conditions: the pawn arrived with a **two-square jump**, and you answer
**on your very next move**. Wait one move and the offer is gone for the rest
of the game.

## The courtesies, and the clock

- **Touch move.** Touch one of your pieces and you must move it if it has a
  legal move. Let go of it and the move is made. Say "I adjust" before you
  straighten a piece.
- **Offering a draw.** Either player may offer; the other may accept or play
  on. Making a move is a polite no.
- **Resigning.** A player who is clearly lost may resign rather than play it
  out. It is a normal, ordinary way for a game to end.
- **Notation.** A letter for the piece and the square it goes to: Nf3. Pawns
  get no letter. `x` is a capture, `+` is check, `#` is checkmate, `O-O` is
  short castling and `O-O-O` long. When two identical pieces could reach the
  same square, add the file of the one that moves: Rae1.
- **The clock.** Each player has a set time for all their moves. Run out and
  you lose, unless your opponent has too little material to mate — then it is
  a draw.
