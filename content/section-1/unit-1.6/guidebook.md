# 1.6 Safety first

Everything in Section 1 was a rule or a pattern. This unit is neither. It is a
**habit**: the same short check, run before every single move, for the rest of
your chess. Beginners do not lose games to clever ideas they have never heard
of. They lose them by leaving a piece where it can be taken, by walking past a
free piece of their opponent's, and by missing a check. The three questions
below catch all three, and they cost about five seconds.

## The three questions

Ask them in this order, every move, even when the answer is obvious.

1. **Is my piece safe?** Look at what your opponent's last move now attacks,
   and at what your own move is about to leave behind. A piece that is attacked
   and undefended is free for them.
2. **Can I take something?** Sweep their side of the board for a piece that is
   attacked and not defended, and for a trade that wins material.
3. **Is my king safe?** Can they give check next move? Is there a mate you have
   not noticed? Is your king still sitting in the middle?

Question one first. Taking a free pawn is no good if it costs you a rook.

## All checks and captures

When you cannot see what to do, list them. Every check you can give, every
capture you can make — and then the same list for your opponent. It is a short
list and it takes a minute, and it finds nearly every mate and nearly every
free piece a beginner misses. Look at **their** list before you pick from
yours.

## Habit level one

This is the whole rulebook for now. It looks dull. It also beats almost
everybody at this level.

- **Castle early.** Get the king off the middle file and the rook into the game.
- **Centre first.** Pawns to the centre, knights and bishops out towards it.
- **Take free pieces.** Attacked and undefended means yours.
- **Never hang your own.** Check what your move leaves behind before you play it.
- **Accept equal trades.** A knight for a bishop is a fine deal. Take it and
  simplify.
- **No gambits.** Do not give away a pawn for "activity" you cannot yet use.
- **No sacrifices.** If you cannot see the forced finish to the end, it is not a
  sacrifice, it is a blunder.

## Before you tap the piece up

Your hand is faster than your eyes. Decide on the move, then run the three
questions **again on the position that move will make**, and only then play it.
That last look is the whole habit.
